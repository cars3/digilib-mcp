Retrieval-Augmented Generation: A Literature Review

September 20, 2026

Literature Review

  • Foundations: Manning, Raghavan & Schütze (2008) and Croft, Metzler & Strohman (2009) supply the classical IR background that the retriever half of any RAG pipeline depends on.
  • Surveys: Fan et al. (2024) map retrieval-augmented LLMs; Peng et al. (2025) survey graph-structured RAG.
  • Evaluation: Es et al. (2024) introduce RAGAs; Salemi & Zamani (2024) propose eRAG, judging retrieval by downstream effect on LLM output.
  • Benchmarking: Chen et al. (2024) build the RGB benchmark, finding models still struggle when retrieved context is noisy.
  • Adaptive retrieval: Jeong et al. (2024) propose Adaptive-RAG, routing queries by complexity.
  • Efficiency: Guo et al. (2025) present LightRAG, combining graph-based indexing with dual-level retrieval.
  • Reliability: Zhang & Zhang (2025) catalogue mitigation techniques for hallucinations across the RAG pipeline.

Open themes: evaluation remains fragmented, retrieval noise is a persistent failure mode, and structured and adaptive retrieval are the most active directions.

Scope

Recent (2024–2025) research on RAG, grounded by two foundational IR textbooks. Papers were selected from a Digilib search spanning OpenAlex, arXiv, Semantic Scholar and Crossref.
